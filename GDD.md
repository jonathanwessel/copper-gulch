# Copper Gulch: game design

Copper Gulch is a free Old West tycoon and tower-defense game. You run towns along a frontier, grow their supply chains, ship goods between them, and hold the road against raiders. It is phone first and installs as an app. It has no ads, no purchases and no tracking.

## Pillars
1. **Every town is a working diorama.** Production chains fill the yard, and a road, a river or a sky lane runs past it that must be defended.
2. **Goods flow between towns**, and the Capital is what they flow toward.
3. **Every number explains itself.** Tapping any rate shows each source and its factor.
4. **The player decides first, and automation takes over later.** It only covers decisions the player has already made by hand.

The Empire map shows the 5-deed gate for Silver Falls in plain words: "Hold 5 deeds. Sign a charter to get more." with the deeds held as have / need. Deeds are kept, never spent (`town.def.found.deeds`, `src/core/empire.js`). Without a charter it never opens: the Gulch's first charter pays 1 deed, and the Ridge's charter pays about 6 once its best wave is near 50. Decision (2026-11-18, the Falls bots): the wait is the Ridge's wave, not the coins. After its charter the Ridge is back past wave 50 in about 35 minutes (7 deeds add 175% to every town's coins) and then climbs slowly, so wave 57 landed the Falls at 6-7 hours. Since group towers grow slower each level (2026.11.18-15) the Ridge's charter pays its deeds at wave 45-47 and the climb back is slower, so the Falls takes wave 52. A coin price big enough to be the wait would sit far past what the purse earns in the 6 minutes the bots save for.

**One purse for the empire** (owner decision, 2026-11-17, save v12). Coins are one balance, shown at the top in every town. Every town and outpost puts its sales, bounties and Call now bonuses into it, and every purchase anywhere spends from it. Each town still counts what it earned itself in this charter (`town.lifetime`), which is what deeds and the Welcome back line read. Tapping the coins shows the town in play by source, then one line for each other town and outpost. Routes move goods only. A charter never touches the purse. The save migration added every town's old coins into the purse.

**Founding a town** (set 2026-11-17, M3). Each later town names what founding it takes (`town.def.found`, `src/core/empire.js`): the town that pays, the best wave it must have reached, and the coins it pays. Iron Ridge: Copper Gulch's best wave 20 (any life) and 15K coins from the shared purse (was 150K in the first pass; the bots never held that much, see Pacing targets). A new town and its outpost each put their start coins into the purse once (Iron Ridge 60, Pine Hollow 150). Once the paying town's best wave is 5 short of the mark, the next town shows as a locked button in the town row at the top, with a red dot once it can be founded; tapping it opens a sheet with what it takes and a Start button. Founding opens the town and its outpost, and takes the player there.

## The Capital (M4, first part shipped 2026-11-18)
- **Building it.** The Capital sits in the middle of the empire map. It comes into sight once Iron Ridge's best wave is 25: its badge and card show the rules (beat wave 30 in Iron Ridge, 25K coins; 150K until 2026-11-23, when the newcomer bot showed it never saved that much: it reaches wave 30 at about 2 hours earning 6.5-10K a minute, so 150K was 15-23 minutes of every coin, and 60K was meant as a 6-9 minute save the goal strip counts down; then 25K, since the save now starts with Hold waves and a held Ridge earns about 3K a minute, so 60K was 19 minutes of holding with nothing to do, clarity fix 3b part 2). On the goal strip: in any other town before the Ridge's wave 30 it says "Tap Map, Iron Ridge, then reach wave 30 there to build the Capital"; from wave 30 the save leads the strip in every town (not an outpost still being won), ahead of the town's own goals, which spend the same purse, and ahead of going back to a town that lost while away: first "Tap Hold waves while you save for the Capital", as the save for Iron Ridge does, then "Save coins to build the Capital" with have / need and the time left (a town that lost while saving stays held until the Capital stands; climbing on, the newcomer lost its vault every few minutes and spent the save on towers; from the sighting at wave 25 the same goes for a town that lost while away, so the climb to wave 30 keeps its coins, clarity fix 3b part 2b); the moment the Ridge beats wave 30 a toast says so once ("Iron Ridge beat wave 30. The Capital costs 25K coins."); once the coins are there, "Tap Map, then tap the Capital to build it" with have / need, Map gets a dot when it can be built, and Build the Capital on its card pays from the purse (`src/core/capital.js`, `src/data/capital.js`). It has no board, road or raiders, so it is never "in play": the player stays on the map and its card is its screen. Once built, the map draws the Capital as a small city on a round stone plaza in place of the surveyed plot (`src/assets/models/capital.js`, layout `CITY` in `src/data/capital.js`): the capitol toward the back (white hall, columns, a copper dome and a flag), the contract board in front of its steps with a sheet pinned up for each offer and a sealed one while a contract runs, and five lots round it, one per institution, the Market Board at the plaza's front gate. A lot shows survey stakes until its institution is built; then its building rises out of the lot (under a second) and joins the city: an open trading hall for the Market Board, a brick depot with a little engine for Transport, a columned stone bank, a brick hall with a clock tower for the University, and an adobe fort with a cannon for the War Office. Every building faces the camera's home side, so none hides behind the dome. The badge floats above the capitol's flag. The plaza stays clear of every town and road (`test/city.test.js`, smoke). The whole city is one merged model, rebuilt only when an institution is built or the board changes.
- **No treasury.** The first sketch had a treasury filled by a 10% tithe on every town. With one purse for the empire (owner decision), a second pile of coins would only hide coins from the player, so it was dropped: institutions are founded with purse coins. The tithe law goes too.
- **Federal contracts.** The board holds 3 offers. Each asks for one made good (never raw) from the open town that makes it, which must have some to spare (what its buildings make, less what its own workshops and the towns it ships to use). The ask is about 4 to 8 minutes of that spare output, rounded to two figures (at least 20 of an intermediate good or 5 of a finished one). No good is on the board twice, and finished goods come up twice as often. A contract pays 1.5 times the good's base price in coins (no market saturation) and Influence: 4 for an intermediate good or 8 for a finished one, for 6 minutes' worth, scaled by the minutes asked.
- **One at a time** (decision: the choice is which one). Take starts one; while it runs, its town sends the good to the Capital by itself, after routes and before the market, from its stock above its workshops' buffer and its tower reserve. The Sell switch does not stop it, like a route. Taking another offer (Switch, two taps) drops the one running, and what it got is lost. Drop (two taps) does the same without taking another. A full contract pays its coins into the purse as the making town's own earnings ("Contracts for the Capital" when coins are tapped, and they count toward that town's deeds), pays its Influence, says so in a toast, and the board fills again. A spot with nothing to ask for looks again every 10 seconds.
- **Rush and big contracts** (clarity fix 3b part 3a, 2026-11-23). Two kinds of contract open as the Capital fills them, each once, with its own tip card: rush contracts after 6 filled, big ones after 15 (`CAPITAL_DEF.contracts.kinds`, set with the bots so each lands in the middle of a wait). While a kind is open and none of it is on the board or running, a new offer is that kind half the time. A **rush** contract asks for 2 to 3 minutes of spare output, pays 1.25 times the Influence for its minutes (and the same coins as a plain one), and has a clock that starts when it is taken: 1.6 times as long as its ask takes at the town's spare rate when it was posted. If the clock runs out, it ends and the goods sent stay at the Capital, unpaid (a toast says so). A **big** contract asks for 14 to 20 minutes and pays what a plain one does for each minute, with no clock, so it runs a long time without a new pick. (First tried with twice the Influence and 1.3 times the coins for a rush one, and 1.25 and 1.2 times for a big one, then with less: on the balance bot's 6 seeds one seed founded Silver Falls at 5.97-5.99 h each time, under its window from 6 h, since the extra Influence goes on War Office upgrades and the Ridge climbs to wave 52 sooner; at 1.5 times for a rush one, once rush opened at 6 contracts, one seed bought an Empire upgrade every 9.2 minutes, under its 10.) The choice: a rush one pays best but needs a town with goods to spare right now; a big one ties up the one contract slot for a long time and needs fewer visits; a plain one keeps the board turning. Why counts: the careful bots went 54-70 minutes with nothing new between the third institution and Call to arms, and contracts fill about every 6-8 minutes in every strategy there, so a count lands at the same time for each. The active bot takes the offer with the most Influence for each minute it asks, a rush one only when its town can fill it in time with 20% to spare.
- **Influence** is the Capital's own currency, shown on its card and its map badge. It buys upgrades in the Empire tree. Map carries a dot while the Capital has no contract running, and while an institution can be founded or an upgrade bought.

## Institutions and the Empire tree (M4, shipped 2026-11-18)
- **Founding.** The Capital's card has two tabs, Contracts and Institutions. Each institution is founded once with coins from the purse: the first costs 40K (50K until 2026-11-23, see below), and each one after it costs 50K times 1.6 for each one founded, whichever it is (40K, 80K, 128K, 205K, 328K; 1.5 times until 2026-11-18, when foremen stopped wasting coins and the fastest seed bought an upgrade every 9.2 minutes), so the order is the player's choice (`src/data/institutions.js`, `src/core/tree.js`). While the first is saved for, a town that lost its boss while the player was away stays held, as during the Capital's own save, and the strip asks to resume it once the building stands (clarity fix 3b part 2d, 2026-11-23: with the Gulch and the Ridge both at a wall, the newcomer of seed 5 went back each time to spend the save on the Gulch's towers and never built it in 30 minutes; at 40K, with that hold, the newcomer builds it 4-15 minutes after the Capital on all 6 seeds). A town that lost a plain wave is not held, since its bounties pay for the save. The Stock Exchange waits for M6.
- **Branches.** Each institution is one branch of the Empire tree: 5 upgrades bought in order with Influence, 10, 15, 25, 40 and 60 (150 a branch; the first was 8 until 2026.11.18-15, when one seed again bought an upgrade every 9.2 minutes). Every upgrade is a named source on every town's stats (the institution's name, so tapping a number shows "Bank"), inside the stat's published cap. The tree is empire-wide: a charter keeps it, a town opened later gets it at once, and only the Convention (M7) will reset it.

| Institution | What its upgrades do |
|---|---|
| Market Board | goods sell for 10%, 15% and 20% more, and contracts pay 25% more Influence twice (the only way to earn Influence faster) |
| Rail Office | outposts make 25% and 50% more, buildings cost 10% and 15% less, every building makes 10% more |
| Bank | coins earned +10%, +10%, +15%, and everything costs 5% less twice |
| University | research 30% faster twice, research costs 25% less, every building makes 10% more, towers reach 10% farther. It will also open Auto-research (Automation) |
| War Office | towers hit 10% and 15% harder, the vault takes 25% more hits, raiders drop 25% more coins, towers shoot 10% faster. It will also open Auto-towers (Automation) |

- **The choice** is where Influence goes first: more Influence later (Market Board), more coins now (Bank), a stronger defense for a boss wall (War Office), or faster research and cheaper building (University, Transport). Each later upgrade of a branch costs more than the first of another, so spreading out and pushing one branch both work.
- **Bots.** The active bot founds them in the order Market Board, Bank, War Office, University, Transport, each once its price is no more than 6 minutes of every town's income (saving for it like the Capital), and buys the cheapest upgrade it can as soon as it has the Influence. Target: an upgrade every 10-15 minutes over the 2 hours after the Capital (`tools/balance.js`, `capitalCheck`).
- **Bots.** The active bot (`tools/balance.js`, `empire` with `capital`) plays on in the Ridge after its 90 minutes there, saves for the Capital once the price is no more than 6 minutes of every town's income (holding the Ridge's waves and switching off every town's foremen, which would spend the one purse), builds it at 2.85-2.9 h of play (3.0-3.4 h at 150K, before 2026-11-23), and then always works on the offer that pays the most Influence: about 5 contracts and 30-45 Influence in 30 minutes.

## Outposts (owner request, 2026-11-17)
- **A won outpost keeps it simple (owner decision, 2026-11-20).** Once won it fights light raids at its winning strength, with no boss, no climb and no drop back. The map label and card say so (Raids, with the time to the next one), and its own wave bar says Raids, not a stuck wave number.
The game starts in a tiny outpost, not a town. Outposts are small raw-goods camps: two raw buildings, a short road, a few stone pads, nothing else. They feed raw goods into the empire-wide market for the rest of the game.

- **Outpost 1, Dry Creek: the start of the game.** (Players see it as Dry Creek, like the other outposts.)
  - Two buildings: a Copper Camp (copper ore) and a Stone Camp (stone, a new raw good). No workshops, no research, no other menus.
  - One tower: the Crossbow, paid with coins plus stone.
  - The road comes in through a dry wash in a gap of a rock ridge (a broken windmill at its mouth), loops once around the camp and leaves the same way. Raiders who finish the loop raid the camp's stockpile, which is the outpost's small vault.
  - About 10 stone pads, inside and outside the loop (6 until 2026-11-20). No mortar pits: outposts never have a Mortar, so the pit rule does not apply to them.
  - Waves 1 to 10, at least 80 seconds apart unless the player calls them early (a careful player who calls wins it in about 9 minutes, a newcomer in about 13), and wave 10 is a bandit wagon (a small boss). Beating wave 10 wins the outpost and opens Copper Gulch, the first town.
- **Each new town opens one more outpost**, so there are outposts between the towns for the whole game. There is no outpost between Dry Creek and Copper Gulch, and none with the Capital. Each one's two raw goods fit the era it opens in:

| # | Outpost | Opens with | Raw goods | Entrance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | Dry Creek Outpost | the start | copper ore, stone | a dry wash through a gap in a rock ridge |
| O2 | Pine Hollow | Iron Ridge | timber, clay (more bricks in the Gulch for the Ridge's boilers) | a logging chute down a wooded slope |
| O3 | Sandy Bend | Silver Falls | sand, quartz | a dry riverbed under a fallen water tower (shipped 2026-11-18: the Sand Wash and the Quartz Diggings, the Crossbow and the Cannon paid with sand, Pine Hollow's waves for now) |
| O4 | Tar Seep | Thunder Mesa | crude oil, rubber | a gully under a leaning oil derrick |
| O5 | Glowrock Camp | Radium Springs | bauxite, pitchblende | a cracked rock face that glows faintly |

- **Later outposts** play like Dry Creek but a little bigger: the Crossbow and the Cannon, both paid with coins and the outpost's own first raw good, and waves 1 to 15 with a small boss at 10 and 15. Winning wave 15 is the outpost's goal. The bots set the exact numbers in each town's box.
- **Before it is won**, an outpost out of play plays on like a town (see Background towns under Defense): its waves keep coming once it has a tower, and a loss holds its waves until the player comes back.
- **They stay useful.** Once won, an outpost keeps working in the background. Its raids stop growing (wave-10 strength, one every 2 minutes or so), and the towers it has fight them on their own. A breach there only takes part of its stockpile and never sets its waves back.
  - **Switching** (owner rule, 2026-11-17: the top bar never grows with the number of towns). The top left holds only the place bar: the town's name, a **Map** button once a second town is open (or a town is in sight), and one **back button** named for the last place in play ("< Dry Creek"), so two places are always one tap apart. Every other switch goes through the empire map: tap a town, then Play (or tap it twice). Towns in sight and ready to start live on the map with their Start card. Map carries a red dot when any other place needs you (it lost and holds its waves, raiders broke in while you were elsewhere, or a town is ready to start), and the back button carries one when its place does. A device with no 3D view gets a Places list from Map instead (Go to each town, and each town in sight with its Start sheet). The last place is kept in the save (`game.prev`, save v13; an old save starts with no back button). Until 2026.11.18-2 a row of town buttons sat at the top instead. The outpost's win card has a Go to Copper Gulch button. A town out of play keeps playing (owner rule, 2026-11-17, as in the old game): its wave clock runs and its towers fight (see Background towns under Defense). A won outpost keeps its raids. (Until 2026.11.18 a town out of play waited for the player; the owner found that wrong.)
  - Its goods go first to the town in play: when a town's workshop is short of a raw good an outpost makes, it draws it from the outposts, up to the workshops' 10-second buffer. Then the other towns' workshops draw the same way (so Pine Hollow's clay reaches the Gulch's Kiln while the player is in Iron Ridge). The rest sells on the shared market, so every outpost adds to the empire's supply of raw goods. Its sales and bounties go into the shared purse, so the town in play can spend them at once, and its income shows as its own line when you tap the coins. Out of play it sells at the town in play's prices (that town's research and perks on prices count), as before the purse. (Before the purse, an outpost's sales were paid to the town in play.)
  - Its camp on the empire map (a small camp past its town) takes you there to build and upgrade, and so does the back button once you have been there.
- **Pine Hollow numbers (tuned by the Ridge bots, 2026-11-17).** The Logging Camp (timber, 0.9 a second per level, 12 coins, built at the start) and the Clay Bank (clay, 0.8 a second, 35 coins, opens at Logging Camp level 2). It starts with 150 coins. The Crossbow costs 20 coins and 6 timber, the Cannon 100 coins and 12 timber. Waves 1 to 15, at least 60 seconds apart, the bandit wagon at 10 and 15 (`waves.bosses`). By the time it opens the Gulch floods the market with timber (about 0.02 coins a unit), so Pine Hollow lives on its bounties: its raiders pay three times the usual bounty, and its waves have 70% of the usual health. Won in 12-20 minutes from getting there, by few strong Crossbows and by many cheap ones.
- **Dry Creek numbers (first pass, the bot box tunes them).** The Stone Camp (stone, 0.8 a second per level, 10 coins, built at the start) and the Copper Camp (copper ore, 0.6 a second per level, 30 coins, opens at Stone Camp level 2). The Crossbow costs 20 coins and 6 stone. Raiders ride a little slower than in the Gulch (the road is short). The bandit wagon has half the war wagon's health. Light raids come every 120 seconds, and a breach takes a quarter of each good in the stockpile.
- **Saves.** A save already in Copper Gulch when outposts arrive gets Dry Creek as won and running, with its two buildings at level 5 and two Crossbows.

## Town maps
- **The lane winds through the town.** Every town's road (and river or sky lane) doubles back, even triples back, in switchbacks between the workshops, so raiders pass the same towers several times and the town is something to defend, not a yard off to one side.
- Workshop plots sit beside the lane; tower spots sit in the bends, where one tower covers two or three passes.
- The vault sits at the lane's end, in the heart of the town.
- **Every lane has a real entrance**, never a bare start. Raiders come out of it, hidden until they are clear of it, and a dust plume (or that lane's own sign) rises behind it a few seconds before each wave, bigger for a boss. `tools/audit.js` fails a lane without an entrance model.
  - Copper Gulch: a narrow slot canyon between tall layered rock walls that run off the board, with an old timber mine portal in a rockfall, a collapsed trestle at the mouth and a signpost with a wanted poster.
  - Iron Ridge: a railway tunnel portal through a granite shoulder, rails running out of it, a signal post and a water tank at the mouth. Its line switches back across the town four times (west, east, west, east) with the workshops in three rows between the passes, 18 pads and 5 mortar pits (12 and 4 until 2026-11-20).
  - Silver Falls (set 2026-11-18): two lanes. The road comes in through a rock cut in the north-east (the canyon model in the Falls' gray granite) and switches back across the town four times like the Ridge's, the last pass ending at the vault. The river comes round a misty bend under a broken stone bridge in the north (`bend`) and winds down the middle of the town to the same vault, so the road crosses it three times on plank bridges that arch over the water (boats pass under them, the steamer folds its stack back to fit, raiders climb over). Workshops stand in three rows of two between the passes, with two more south of the vault; the pads between a workshop and the river reach two passes of the road and the water. 19 pads (12 until 2026-11-20) and 3 mortar pits, each well back from both lanes (4 until 2026-11-18: the east pit by the rock cut gave the Mortar every raider first, and with it half the town's damage). Every third raider comes by boat (`waves.boatEvery`); boats ride only the river and hurt the vault like riders. Every tower can hit boats, and the Mortar's shells land on the water too.
  - Sandy Bend: a dry riverbed between sandy banks under a fallen wooden water tower (`riverbed`). Thunder Mesa: airships come out of a cloud bank. Radium Springs: out of a glowing fissure.
- **Outpost maps** are smaller: the road enters, loops once around the camp and leaves, past the camp's few pads, with the stockpile (the vault) where the loop closes. They have a real entrance like every lane, and no mortar pits.
- **More pads (owner, 2026-11-20: "Add more tower pads per map").** About half again as many pads on every map: outposts 10-11 (Dry Creek 10, Pine Hollow and Sandy Bend 11), towns 22-23 (Copper Gulch and Iron Ridge 23 with a fifth mortar pit, Silver Falls 22 with its 3 pits). The pads between the passes and inside the bends were already there, so the new ones stand along the outer sides of the road, at its corners and where it comes in, each clear of the road, the lots, the vault and every other pad, and at least 1.5 apart (about 48 px on a phone at the home view). They are added at the end of `board.spots`, so a saved tower keeps its pad, and the pits are listed by index in `board.pits`.
- **Extra towers cost more.** A town's normal towers up to `board.crowd` (the pads it had before, 6 to 12) cost the list price. Each one after that costs 25% more coin and goods than the one before (`EXTRA_TOWER`); every upgrade and the Mortar cost the same. The goods count too, since in an outpost late in the game coins are plenty and timber is what limits towers. A stone pad's card says "You have 12 towers here. The prices below go up with each new tower." and a card explains it the first time. Why: with every new pad as cheap as the rest, the swarm bot won Pine Hollow 40% ahead of min-max; this keeps the new room useful while a few strong towers stay as good a plan as many cheap ones.
- **Mortar pits.** Every town map has 3 to 5 far pads, set well back from the lane and the buildings: outside a normal tower's reach of most of the lane, but with several passes of the lane inside the Mortar's ring. They look different (a ring of sandbags). Only long-range towers (the Mortar) can be built on them, and long-range towers go only there. `tools/audit.js` fails a town without them (outposts are exempt).

## Supply chains
Goods go from raw to intermediate to finished. "<-" marks a good shipped in from another town.

**A stalled workshop says why** (clarity fix 6, 2026-11-24). A built workshop that makes under half its rate for about 20 seconds, short of a good (or held back because the towers get that good first), is stalled. Its card says what it lacks and which building to upgrade ("Not enough steel. Upgrade the Steel Mill."), and the row of its good on the Goods sheet says "Waits for steel from the Steel Mill". While a goal needs that workshop or a good it makes, the goal strip first asks for the building that feeds it, following the chain back (Boiler Shop, Steel Mill, Blast Furnace). When the maker is in another town, the strip sends the player there ("Tap Map, Copper Gulch, then upgrade the Kiln. The Boiler Shop needs more bricks"), and that town's strip asks for it ("Tap the Kiln and upgrade it. Iron Ridge needs more bricks"). The step goes once the workshop runs again.

- **Copper Gulch**
  - Raw: timber (Lumber Camp), copper ore (Copper Mine), clay (Clay Pit).
  - Intermediate: planks (Sawmill, 2 timber), copper (Smelter, 2 ore + 1 timber as fuel), bricks (Kiln, 2 clay + 1 timber).
  - Finished: tools (Toolsmith, planks + copper), wagons (Wagon Works, 2 planks + tools + copper).
- **Iron Ridge** (set 2026-11-17, tuned by the Ridge bots)
  - Buildings: Quarry (stone, built at the start), Iron Mine (Quarry 3), Coal Pit (Quarry 5), Blast Furnace (Iron Mine 5 and Coal Pit 3), Steel Mill (Blast Furnace 3, 1200 coins), Boiler Shop (Steel Mill 3, 4000 coins), Rolling Mill (Steel Mill 5), Rolling Mill 3000 coins (was 12K), Locomotive Works (Rolling Mill 5, Boiler Shop 5, 15K coins; was Rolling Mill 10 and 80K, and the bots never reached it in 2.5 hours). The Ridge's goal strip leads through the chain to the Locomotive Works and sends the player to the Gulch for a Toolsmith. The Boiler Shop comes early so the bricks route from the Gulch matters in the Ridge's first hour (it was Steel Mill 5 and 15K, and the bots never reached it).
  - Towers pay with Ridge goods: Crossbow stone, Cannon iron ore, Frost Lens coal and stone, Still iron ore and coal, Mortar pig iron and stone.
  - Research: Steam Drills, Hot Blast, Iron Plating, Rifled Barrels, Railroad Rewards, Freight Depot, and Bessemer Converter (Steel Mill +60%, one more coal per steel: the trade-off). It opens with the Blast Furnace. Three more show up as the Ridge climbs toward the Capital, each with a price: Telescope Sights at wave 27 (towers reach 10% farther, fire 15% slower, 2 levels), Steam Hammers at wave 32 (buildings work 15% faster, each level costs 15% more, 3 levels) and Bounty Office at wave 37 (raiders drop 40% more coins and have 10% more health, 2 levels). The numbers come from runs with each item on and off: at 15% farther and 10% slower the Sights let the bots open Silver Falls 0.6-1.2 h sooner, under its window; at these numbers they change nothing in the Falls time, so the item is a trade, not a free gain. Bounty Office first cost 10% of sale prices, which Auto-research then kept raising in an idle empire (idle sales fell to 89% of the first hour). They fill the hour the bots spent with nothing new between the Ridge's routes and the Capital (clarity fix 3b part 1).
  - Raw: iron ore, coal, stone.
  - Intermediate: pig iron (2 iron + coal), steel (pig iron + coal).
  - Finished: rails (2 steel), boilers (steel + 2 bricks <- Gulch), locomotives (boiler + 2 rails + tools <- Gulch).
  - The goal strip (clarity fix 3b part 2): after the Blast Furnace and Pine Hollow it walks the Steel Mill, the Boiler Shop, the Rolling Mill, the Gulch's Toolsmith and the Locomotive Works, a level at a time while a lot is shut, and asks for towers as the best wave climbs: a path at wave 7, a tower at level 10 at wave 12, 5 towers at level 5 at 17, 5 at level 8 at 22, 3 at level 10 at 27, a tower at level 15 at 28 (31 until clarity fix 3b part 2b: on seeds 4-5 the newcomer climbed from 27 to 30 for 11-12 minutes with its upgrades spread over 23 towers, and one tower at 15 got seed 2 past its wall at wave 29), and 5 at level 10 at 38. Once the Capital stands, the empire's next steps are "Tap Map, then tap the Capital and take a contract", then its first building with the price as have / need; while that building is short of coins and no contract runs, the strip asks for another contract, since each one pays toward it. Tapping the strip opens the card of the building, pad or tower it names, where the upgrade's price shows as have / need.
- **Silver Falls** (set 2026-11-18, tuned by the Falls bots the same day)
  - Buildings: Sand Pit (sand, built at the start), Quartz Mine (Sand Pit 3), Water Wheel (Sand Pit 4), Glassworks (Sand Pit 5 and Water Wheel 1), Wire Mill (Glassworks 3), Bulb Works (Wire Mill 3), Dynamo Works (Wire Mill 5), Telegraph Works (Bulb Works 5 and Dynamo Works 3).

When the workshops want more than the grid makes, every workshop on it runs at the same share (`gridOf` in `src/core/economy.js`) and its card says "Not enough power. Every workshop here runs slower. Upgrade the Water Wheel." The Water Wheel's card says how much power the workshops use. So the choice is how far ahead of the workshops to keep the Wheel. Its price in the data (0.5) counts only for the chain rule.
  - Towers pay with Falls goods: Crossbow sand, Cannon quartz, Frost Lens quartz and sand, Still sand and glass, Mortar glass and quartz. Quartz is used for towers until Radium Springs' radios.

- **The Capital** makes nothing. It posts federal contracts for finished goods from every town.
- **Routes** (set 2026-11-17, M3). A town ships the goods it makes to every later town that imports them, by wagon, automatically: as much as the importing town's workshops are short of (up to their 10-second buffer), from what the maker has above its own workshops' buffer and tower reserve. No coins change hands, since every town spends from the one purse (until the purse in save v12 the importer paid the maker the market price). The maker counts what it sent (`town.sent`). Imported goods are never sold again. Routes go on in every town, in play or not, and the Sell switch does not stop them (it only stops market sales). The building card of a workshop that uses an import says where it comes from, and when it runs short, which building to upgrade in the other town. Decision: no route levels to buy; the choice is how far to grow the Gulch's Kiln and Toolsmith for the Ridge. The empire map draws the wagons.
- **Outposts** make only raw goods (see Outposts). They add supply of goods a town may also make (ore, stone, timber, clay...), and towns draw from them before the market.

**Chain rules** (checked by `tools/audit.js`):
- each step is worth 1.3 to 1.6 times its inputs at base price;
- workshops are balanced at equal levels;
- every imported good has exactly one producing town (outposts do not count, they only add raw supply);
- every town's top good needs at least one import (outposts are exempt, they make only raw goods);
- no town depends on a later town.

## Market
- **Prices.** One empire market. Price = base × saturation × event.
  - Saturation is `rec / (rec + sellRate / depth)`. It recovers over time, and each good shows a depth bar.
  - Selling a spread of goods, and selling higher up the chain, pays best.
- **Booms and busts** (2026.11.22-3, `BOOM` in `src/core/market.js`): one good at a time. A new one starts every 3 to 5 minutes, on a good some town or outpost makes, never the same good twice in a row, and lasts 90 seconds. A boom makes it sell for 1.6 times its price, a bust for 0.6 times (a bust a little under half the time). The clock starts once a second town is open, so the first comes 8 minutes after Iron Ridge is founded: Dry Creek and the Gulch's first hour have none, so a new player learns the core first and the first hour's pacing stays as it was (decided 2026.11.22-3: with booms from minute 8, the min-max bot's Gulch wave 20 flipped on the war wagon on two seeds). Its good's chip on the goods strip gets a green up arrow or a red down arrow in its corner (the chip keeps its size), and its row on the Goods sheet says "Boom 1:12" or "Bust 0:45" with the time left. An explainer shows the first time one falls on a good the strip shows. Foremen leave booms out of their sums (a level lasts, a boom does not). The market keeps its own random stream, so a town's luck never moves it.
- **Town orders** (2026.11.22-4, `ORDERS` in `src/data/orders.js`, `src/core/orders.js`): buyers in each town (not an outpost) ask for a load of a good the town makes, about 3 to 6 minutes of its spare output, to be filled within twice that time once taken. Each town keeps 2 offers on its board, in an Orders part at the top of its Goods sheet, and a new one comes about 45 seconds after one is taken; an offer nobody takes is replaced after 10 minutes. They open a minute after a second town beats its wave 10 (so after Iron Ridge's wave 10 for every strategy, and after the first boom), so the first hour and the Ridge's first waves play as before. Decision (2026.11.22-4): a clock of 10 minutes from the second town opened them while the player was still in Pine Hollow, and the active bot's Ridge wave 10 came at 11.5 min on one seed (window 7-10).
  - **One at a time per town, taken by the player.** Take starts it, and the town then sends the good by itself, from what it has above its workshops' buffer and its tower reserve, after routes and the Capital's contract and before the market sells any, so what an order needs is never sold. Nothing but the player takes an order: the goal strip and Auto never do (automation only takes over decisions the player made by hand; an Auto switch for orders can come later).
  - **Pay**: what the order's whole load would fetch on the market if it were sold over the order's time (base times the saturation the load itself causes, and the town's price bonus, no boom or bust), times 1.3, and up to half more with reputation. What an order takes counts on the market's sales rate like a sale, so the next offer for that good pays less. So an order always beats selling the same goods, but never by more than its premium. Decision (2026.11.25-9): priced at today's price, an order for minutes of a big town's output paid far past what the market could take, and a town that held its goods (Off or Booms only) filled order after order from its pile at full price: the sell-mode probe's active bot on Booms only earned 23-52M coins by the end of the Ridge run against about 1M. Load pricing brought it to 1.0-1.2M against 0.92-1.0M on Always. Decision (2026-11-22): at 1.2 times the base price the active bot's orders paid 16-20M coins a run and Silver Falls came 0.6 h sooner, near the bottom of its window.
  - **Reputation** (0 to 100, per town, kept through a charter): a filled order adds 8 (a rush order 12), a missed one or one given up takes 15. Giving up, or taking another offer while one runs, takes two taps, since it costs reputation. The goods already sent stay with the buyer.
  - **Rush orders** come from reputation 60, 4 offers in 10: a smaller ask (2 to 3 minutes of output) with 1.4 times its time and 1.6 times the pay.
  - The trade-off: an order pays more than the market and frees the market for the rest, but it holds that good's spare until it is full, and one that is too big for the town's output costs reputation. Taking one a town cannot make in time is the mistake to learn.
  - Bots: the active and strategy players take, in every town, the offer that pays the most a minute that their spare output fills with 20% room (`tendOrders` in `tools/balance.js`); the newcomer and idle players take none. `ordersCheck` asks every active seed to fill at least 5, miss at most a quarter, and reach reputation 60 somewhere.
- **Sell modes per good**: Always (the default), skip busts, booms only and Off, picked on the Goods sheet (tap Sell spare on a good's row; the building card's switch goes between Always and Off). On screen every mode is its price picture and one line about what it does ("Sells spare goods whenever you have some.", "Stops selling while the price is down.", "Sells only while the price is up.", "Never sells spare goods."), and every Sell spare switch shows its mode's picture: the names of the middle two were flagged in three reviews (clarity fix 8d), and with names on only two modes the picker had two shapes (clarity fix 8h). The price words are the boom line's own, up and down. Every mode keeps the workshops' buffer and the tower reserve (and orders, once they come). Only market sales wait: routes, the Capital's contract and an outpost's goods for the towns go on. The trade-off: Skip busts and Booms only hold goods back to sell them at once later, and selling a lot at once lowers the price of the rest, so they pay best on a good the market is not flooded with. Booms only on a good that seldom booms holds it for a long time. The idle and new players leave every good on Always, and the pacing targets hold that way.
  - **What each mode is for** (Market depth part 2b, 2026.11.25-10, `tools/mode-probe.js`, the active bot on 6 seeds to the end of the Ridge run): Booms only pays in a town whose orders are open. Its stock waits there, so each order the player takes fills at once at the order's premium, and only a boom sells the rest; set once orders open, the empire earned 16-42% more coins than on Always (about 30% on the middle seed), with the Ridge's wave 10 at the same minute on every seed. Set before orders open it sells almost nothing and the Ridge's wave 10 comes 4 minutes later. Off does the same for orders but never sells in a boom. Skip busts is the safe choice: a bust is 90 seconds on one good of about ten, so it keeps under 1% of sales from going cheap, and it never costs more than that either. Decision: keep it as that, since a deeper or longer bust big enough to matter would cut every Always player's income, the pacing targets' own play.
  - A pile a mode held back (Skip busts after a bust, Booms only in a boom) lets out over about 30 seconds, never all in one step (`HOLD` in `src/core/market.js`): sold at once, it crashed its own price, and Skip busts paid a little less than Always on most seeds.
  - **No buy-and-sell loop earns coins** (the M6a target): the market only buys from the player, never sells to them, and a boom or bust only moves the price of goods made by buildings. `test/boom.test.js` checks that no market action turns coins into goods, and that any buy the market gets later (the Stock Exchange, part 3) loses coins on a buy and sell at once.
- **The tower reserve** (from the first minute; bigger since 2026.11.19-15, owner: "auto sell needs to be less aggressive and keep more inventory. It's a pain to build towers"). Per good the town keeps the next two upgrades of every tower that uses it (the path choice counts as one), plus two of the costliest open tower that uses it while pads are free (no more than the free pads), and the same for free mortar pits. Auto-sell, routes and the Capital's contract also leave at least 2 minutes of the town's own output of that good, but never more than twice the towers' need. Workshops leave the towers' need alone too (not the 2-minute part), so the Smelter never eats the ore a Cannon waits for; a workshop held back that way says "Your towers get the ore first" on its card and is not shown as a problem. One switch per town on the Goods sheet, "Keep goods for · 2 tower upgrades / 4 tower upgrades" (4 keeps twice the levels and towers; the owner asked for "Stock for towers", renamed after the screenshot reviews so it says how much, while each building card's "Keep timber for towers" says which good), and each good still has its own "Keep for towers" switch, on by default, for players who want to sell or use everything. Building cards and the Goods list say how much is kept; a tower card short of a good says how much there is, about how long it takes, and which building makes it. `tools/reserve.js` proves it with a saver bot that holds its coins for 4 minutes and then buys in a burst, as the owner plays.
- **Federal contracts** at the Capital pay **Influence** plus coins. They are the main Influence source and the reason to ship goods.
- **The Stock Exchange** comes late and is optional. Each town has a share, and prices follow its income and news.
  - A 1% spread means no buy-and-sell loop can earn coins.
  - Holding a majority gives +25% income in that town.

## Defense
- **Towers.** Nine, unlocked by era: crossbow, cannon, frost lens, snake oil still, mortar, tesla coil, gatling, barracks, drone helipad.
  - Each costs coins plus that town's own goods, so the chain feeds the defense. In Copper Gulch (tuned for variety, last on 2026-11-17): the Crossbow takes timber, the Cannon copper ore, the Frost Lens planks and clay, the Snake Oil Still copper ore and planks, the Mortar copper and bricks. So four kinds can be built in the first 7 minutes, before the Smelter, and the Mortar waits for wave 12 and the Smelter and Kiln. In Iron Ridge the Still takes stone and coal, so its high levels never hold back the iron the Blast Furnace needs (2026.11.21-8). Level-1 damage per coin stays within about a third of the Crossbow's, so pricier kinds win where pads run out. A raider carries one dose of poison and one chill at a time, so a second Still or Frost Lens on the same stretch of road adds less than the first.
  - **Each kind has a job** (set 2026-11-17, M2 Tower balance), shown on its card as "Best against": the Crossbow hits Horsemen twice as hard; the Still's poison is weak on unarmored raiders and 2.2 times as strong on armored ones (Brutes and the wagons), and it grows 15% a level where a hit grows 30%, so one maxed Still can't carry a town; from level 10 its poison also eats armor (since 2026.11.21-8): a raider carrying its poison or Slow Rot loses half its armor against every tower's hits, and the damage breakdown counts what that adds for the Still, so a Still among strong towers keeps a job late in the game, when its own poison has fallen behind; the Mortar hits the wagons 2.5 times as hard and has the widest blast, for packed groups. The Cannon (groups) and the Frost Lens (slows, chips every raider in range) keep their old jobs. No kind may do more than about 35% of the damage for any strategy bot, in Copper Gulch and in Silver Falls, and every kind must lead some waves for at least one (`tools/balance.js`).
  - **Growth per level** (set 2026-11-18, M5): a hit grows 1.3 times a level for the Crossbow, which hits one raider, and slower for the towers that hit a group: the Cannon and the Frost Lens 1.24, the Mortar 1.19 (`grow` in `src/data/towers.js`). The Cannon grew 1.25 until 2026-11-20, when the new pads let the economy player's one Cannon do 37% of its Silver Falls damage. A level 5 Mortar now hits 70% as hard as before and a level 5 Cannon 85%, which the Gulch bots hardly felt. By Silver Falls, where the purse raises every tower to level 13 or so, it keeps a group tower from carrying the town: at 1.3 for all, the Mortar did 50-63% of the Falls' damage for every strategy.
  - **Price per level** (set 2026-11-25, M6a): a level costs 1.55 times the coins of the one before up to level 20, and 1.45 times after it; goods grow 1.35 times a level all the way (`PER_LEVEL`, `HIGH_LEVEL` in `src/data/towers.js`). At 1.55 all the way each level past the fork bought a hit about 15% dearer than the last, so a few towers pushed to 22-27 (the min-max way) paid about twice the many-towers price for their damage and reached Silver Falls 29% behind.
  - Paths open at level 5.
  - **Deep Freeze** (the Frost Lens's first path) freezes the raiders in reach for 0.8 s on every third pulse. A raider that thaws walks for 1.2 s before any lens can freeze it again (`FREEZE` in `src/data/towers.js`; set 2026-11-18). The idle bot found 11 lenses on Iron Ridge's road holding raiders frozen for half an hour, so one wave took 40 minutes and the town earned almost nothing; now a raider is frozen at most about 40% of the time however many lenses overlap (2.4 s, a quarter, was tried first and left the bots' Ridge too weak at its wall to save for institutions).
- **The Mortar** is the long-range tower. If a normal tower reaches from 0 to R, the Mortar reaches from 0.5R to 2R: it cannot fire at raiders inside 0.5R (its dead zone).
  - Slow fire. A high, arcing shell with a visible flight time, a marker on the ground where it will land, and splash damage on landing.
  - It lobs, so it cannot hit airships. It can hit boats.
  - Its card and the selection highlight draw both circles, with the dead zone shaded.
  - Paths at level 5: Siege Mortar (a bigger blast, reach 2.4R) or Shrapnel (a smaller blast that leaves burning ground, which slows and hurts for a few seconds).
  - Copper Gulch opens it at wave 12, for coins plus copper and bricks. Later towns pay with their own goods (Iron Ridge: pig iron).
  - In the background battle its damage counts only for the share of the lane inside its ring; Auto-towers treats it like any tower.
- **Waves** come in types (mixed, rush, swarm, heavy), with weather, and a boss every 10th wave.
  - **Boss waves are gates** (owner rule, 2026-11-17: "They should be a gate I can't get past"). A boss wave is beaten only when its boss is killed, in every town and outpost, in play and away. If the boss reaches the vault or stockpile, the wave is lost like a fallen vault: back 2 waves, the purse's share, the vault refills. The next wave number never passes an unbeaten boss. The waves then hold, in play too, so the player decides when to try again: a card says "The war wagon reached your vault. You dropped back to wave 18 and lost 120 coins." and "Your waves are on hold. Build more towers, then tap Resume waves." Call now also tries again at once. A won outpost's light raids bring no boss, so they have no gate (a breach there takes goods, as before).
  - Boss numbers (retuned 2026-11-18 for the gate, by the bots): a boss's health grows slower than its wave's (`bossGrowth` 0.4: the wave-10 health times the wave's growth since wave 10 to the power 0.4), so a careful player who gets ready can stop each one; at the full growth every bot lost the wave-20 war wagon over and over with half its health left. War wagon 12 times a raider's health (was 20), bandit wagon 8 (was 10), armored train 19 (was 26, then 21 until 2026.11.18-15, when group towers began to grow slower each level and the active bot lost the train once on half the seeds). The bots now get ready for a boss (their towers a level higher while a boss wave is next) and after a loss build a level higher and one tower more, then tap Resume waves after 20 seconds (the newcomer after 45).
  - **Boss stages and the boss bar** (owner, 2026-11-22: "when a war wagon came, there was an indicator at the top of the screen that showed the health of that boss as well as the stage the boss was in"). Each boss changes once as it is hurt, and the model shows it (`stages` in `src/data/foes.js`): the war wagon's crew of 3 jumps off at half health and runs on (Wagon crew, half a raider's health, fast) while the wagon slows to 0.6 of its pace; the bandit wagon's 2 horsemen ride off at half health and it slows the same way; the armored train's plates fall off at half health, so it goes 1.15 times as fast with armor 0.15 (was 0.4); the ironclad steamer lets off steam at a third of its health and goes 1.1 times as fast (first tried at 1.35 for the train and 1.5 for the steamer, which made them walls for the economy bot: its Ridge wave 10 at 11-13 min on three seeds against 7-9 with no stages; a steamer at 1.25 still won Silver Falls wave 10 on seed 1). The riders that jump off count toward the wave, pay their bounty and hit the vault like any raider, but only the boss decides the gate. The background battle plays the same steps, so stages count away and offline too. While a boss is on the road a slim bar sits at the foot of the top bar's left column (so it moves nothing above it): its picture and name, its health as have / most, its armor and its stage in a few words ("Crew jumped off"). It keeps one width; a tap opens the boss's raider card; when it falls the bar says "War wagon down!" for a moment and goes. Trade-off by design: the wagons get easier to finish but spill fast raiders, so a defense needs some reach near the vault; the train swaps armor for speed, so armor-piercing towers matter less after half; the steamer gets harder at the end.
  - The wave pace: the next wave comes a short rest after the last one is cleared, and never sooner than a set time after the last one started (Copper Gulch: 7 s and 58 s; Dry Creek: 80 s). Call now starts the next wave at once and pays bonus coins for the time skipped. It is never locked (owner rule, 2026-11-17): it works from the first second of every break, in every town and outpost. What keeps a strong defense from racing far ahead is the wave, not the button: calling in the last 40% of the wait is free, and the share of the wait skipped before that (rush, 0 to 1) brings (1 + 2 x rush) times the wave's raiders, copies of its own in the same order, with one boss still last (Iron Ridge: 1 + 4 x rush, `rushMore`; 3.5 until 2026-11-18, when Deep Freeze stopped holding raiders for good and a seed reached the Ridge's wave 10 in 6.6 min). A wave lasts about as long as its raiders take to ride out, so a bigger wave takes longer and racing saves less time. The bonus is half what it was (`RUSH` in `src/core/defense.js`). The first wave and waves beaten before a charter come at normal size however early. Decision (2026-11-17): tougher raiders (up to 2 times the health) were tried first and did not work, because a strong defense killed them just as fast and raced to wave 10 in 6.5 min against 8.7 for economy first. Before this the button was gray until about half the wait was gone (`CALL_OPEN`); the owner found that "dumb".
- **The raider card** (owner, 2026-11-22: "Can I click on a mob to see the current buffs and debuffs?"). A tap on any raider opens a small card: its picture and name, health as a bar with have / most, armor as the share of each hit it blocks with what poison ate off it, each effect on it now with a mark, a word and the seconds left (Frozen, Poisoned, Rotting with no end, Spreads poison, On fire, Slowed, Brittle), the coins it pays and the tower that hits it hardest. The card follows the raider while the game runs, with the selection ring on it, and says "Bandit down!" or "It got to the vault." for a moment when it goes. The map slides only when the raider walks under the card, so its ring stays in sight. Nothing new is drawn over raiders by default. One core function (`foeStatus`) gives every value, and the bots count what fallen raiders carried with it. No raider gives others anything yet; one that does (a shield bearer's aura) adds its effect there.
- **Each lane has its own threats**: boats on the river (the Riverboat, 2.2 times a raider's health with light armor, and the ironclad steamer, Silver Falls' boss at 18 times a raider's health and heavy armor (20 until 2026.11.18-15, when the active bot lost it once on one seed); both shipped 2026-11-18), airships in the sky (only Tesla and drones reach them), and jamming in Radium Springs.
- **The vault** is each town's health. When it falls, the town drops back 2 waves and the purse loses 10%, but never more than that town earned in about the last 5 minutes (`town.recent`, fading with a 5-minute time constant). Decision (2026-11-17, with the shared purse): a breach in a young town must not wipe out savings the whole empire made, and a busy town still feels it. A won outpost loses goods instead, as before.
  - **It mends a little** (clarity fix 3b part 2c, 2026-11-23): each wave beaten gives back a tenth of the vault's full strength (2 of 20 hits, 2.5 of 25), never past full, in play and away alike (`WAVE.mend` and `mendHits`, in `waveEnd`, which the background battle's runs end in too). A lost wave mends nothing; its fall refills the vault as before. The Vault sheet says it in words, "Each wave you beat mends the vault a little." (clarity fix 8g: "+2½ hits" beside a heart that counts whole hits was flagged twice; whole hits were tried, 3 for a 25-hit vault, and tipped the Ridge's frost share to 36.0% and newcomer seed 1's Capital, so the mend stays a tenth and the sheet drops the number). Decision: before it, a vault never refilled between waves, so a newcomer at a wall with 4 to 8 hearts of 20 spent every coin on towers and could not save for a building goal for 13-14 minutes (the Ridge's waves 24-29 on seed 1). With it the playthrough's longest quiet stretch after hour 1 on seeds 1-6 reads 8, 10, 6, 6, 11, 9 min (14, 10, 8, 8, 8, 8 without), the minutes over the 10-minute limit summed over the 6 seeds fall from 4 to 1, and the Capital opens sooner on 5 of the 6 seeds.
- **Background towns** (owner rule, 2026-11-17: "the timer till the next wave stops. That's not how it worked in the previous version"). Every town and outpost keeps playing while the player is not looking at it: out of play, on the empire map, and in time away, closed or in the background.
  - Out of play, its waves run step by step exactly as in play, only not drawn. During time away the first 10 minutes are stepped the same way; after that, in the coarse chunks, each wave is played out on a copy of the town (`waveRun` in `src/core/defense.js`) and its result applied: the bounties of the raiders that fell, the vault hit by those that got through, in order. Decision: a played-out copy instead of the old game's tower-strength formula, so the background battle can never disagree with the battle on screen (a test checks they match). Runs are kept per town and reused when the same wave meets the same towers, so a won outpost's raids cost almost nothing all day.
  - A town with no towers yet waits for the player (decision: a town just opened is never raided before the player has had a look). A won outpost keeps its raids whatever it has.
  - Each town's Hold waves still parks it.
  - **Auto-hold after a loss.** When a town away loses its vault or its boss wave, it pays the loss (back 2 waves, the purse's share), then holds its own waves until the player goes there and taps Resume waves or Call now. So it pays at most once while away, never in a loop. A won outpost never drops back, so it never holds. In the town in play a fallen vault does not hold the first time (the player is watching), but a lost boss does (see Boss waves are gates), and so does a second fall in a row with no new best wave (see At its wall).
  - **At its wall** (the Gulch's loss loop, 2026-11-25). A town that loses twice in a row with no new best wave between, in play or away, is at its wall (`atWall` in `src/core/defense.js`). The second loss holds its waves in play too (a fallen vault in play held nothing before, so the newcomer's Ridge fell 17-33 times on its climb to wave 52), and the strip there says "Tap a tower and upgrade it, then tap Resume waves" (the loss card says the same; its pads are full by then, and more towers at level 1 lost twice already). Away, the strip stops sending the player back to resume it, its label reads On hold with no red dot, and its card says "Back to wave 29 again. Tap Play Copper Gulch, upgrade its towers, then tap Resume waves." It keeps making and selling its goods. Only when nothing else is on the strip does it say "Tap Map, Copper Gulch, then upgrade its towers". A new best wave starts the count again. Decision: before it, the strip sent the newcomer back to its Gulch after every loss during the Ridge's climb, a few upgrades went in, and the Gulch fell again a few minutes later, 32-63 times on each seed, about two thirds of the 106-583K coins lost between the Ridge's charter and Silver Falls. Holding it at its wall costs only its bounties from the two waves it would win back, much less than a fall (up to 5 minutes of the town's income). With it the median lost on that climb is under 100K, and Silver Falls still opens on all 6 seeds.
  - **The trip back to a wall** (2026-11-25, How long a town waits at its wall). Held at its wall, the newcomer's Gulch sat 5.5 to 7 hours on towers mostly at level 1, while each of its upgrades cost under 100 coins. Now once a town has waited 30 minutes at its wall since its last loss (`WALL_WAIT`), and the purse can pay for its 8 cheapest upgrades, the strip says "Tap Map, Copper Gulch, then upgrade its towers" between other steps, and its map label gets the red dot (`wallReady`, `needsYou` 'wall'). There the strip counts the upgrades made since it held, "0 / 8 upgrades" (`WALL_UPS`, `town.lost.ups`). A trip that ends in another loss at the same wave waits 30 minutes again, so it never loops. Tried on 6 seeds: with 20 minutes and 5 upgrades Silver Falls opened 20 minutes later at the median and one seed at 595 of its 600, with 30 and 8 it opens as before (median 485 against 484). A save the strip asks for (the Capital or the next town) still holds every town first.
  - It says so: a toast wherever the player is ("The armored train got through in Iron Ridge. Its waves are on hold until you go there."), the Welcome back line after a cold start, a dot on the Map button, and on the map its badge's dot, "On hold" in place of its coins a second, and a line on its card ("Tap Play, then Resume waves.", over the Play Copper Gulch button). Hold waves reads Resume waves while held, and the wave bar says on hold in place of a countdown.
  - With "Keep trying the boss" on (Automation), a town that lost a boss wave, or whose vault fell, never sits on hold: it plays the wave before the one it lost again and again, for its bounty, and tries the lost wave again once a tower has gone up a level, so Auto-towers can break through.
- **Charters**: a town's deeds depend on its best wave, so defense matters for prestige.

## Progression: three layers
| Layer | Currency | Buys | Reset by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Town | the shared purse (one for the empire) | buildings, towers, town research (6 to 8 items per town, plus up to 3 that show up later), foremen | that town's charter, which pays deeds |
| Empire | Influence, plus coins from the one purse (the treasury and its tithe were dropped with the purse, 2026-11-18) | founding each institution once with coins; its levels are nodes in one Empire tree; laws in pairs, locked until the Convention | the Convention, which pays Legacy |
| Perks | deeds and Legacy | deed perks and Legacy perks, on one Perks screen | never |

### Town research
- Each town has 6 to 8 research items from the start, plus up to 3 that show up when its best wave reaches a mark (the Gulch's Powder Kegs at 26, the Ridge's three from 27 to 37), so something new opens in the middle of a long climb; a save already past the mark gets them at once. Each has levels, a coin price that triples per level, and a wait that doubles per level (from 20 s). One item runs at a time. Research keeps going while you are away.
- Research opens in Copper Gulch when the Sawmill is built (about 4 minutes in), with a Research button beside Goods.
- Every bonus is a named source on a stat, so the explain view lists it ("Sharp Saws research").
- Copper Gulch:

| Item | Levels | Each level | Starts at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sharp Saws | 5 | Lumber Camp and Sawmill +25% | 200 coins |
| Deep Shafts | 5 | Copper Mine and Clay Pit +25% | 400 |
| Thick Walls | 5 | vault +25% | 300 |
| Gunsmith | 5 | towers hit 15% harder | 500 |
| Wanted Posters | 5 | raiders pay 30% more when they fall | 600 |
| Market Stalls | 5 | goods sell for 10% more | 800 |
| Hot Furnaces | 2 | Smelter and Kiln +60%, but they burn one more timber per unit per level (a trade-off) | 1500 |
| Powder Kegs | 3 | Cannon and Mortar blasts 20% wider. Shows up at best wave 26, with a toast, so something new opens between the Mortar and the first charter | 2500 |

### Foremen
- A building you have raised to level 10 by hand can hire a foreman, for 3 times its next level's price.
- A working foreman buys that building's next level whenever it costs half the coins on hand or less, so the other half stays for towers and research. A switch on the building card turns it off.
- A foreman waits on a level that would not pay (set 2026-11-18, `foremanWait` in `src/core/foremen.js`): while its building is short of an input ("The foreman is waiting for more clay."), and while the next level would take over an hour to earn back its price at today's prices ("The foreman is waiting. Timber sells for very little now. No workshop needs more timber.", or "...The next level would take over an hour to earn back its price."). One more unit sold counts at base price times saturation squared (`marginal` in `src/core/market.js`), since selling more also lowers the price of the rest. A level is always wanted, whatever it pays, when a workshop in the town, in a town it ships the good to, or (for an outpost) in any town is short of the good, or when the towers keep more of it than the yard holds. The idle bot found foremen on camps whose timber and stone sold at 1,000 a second spending millions on levels that earned nothing.
- A foreman also waits, short of that good, when the next level would use more of a shipped good than the town that makes it can send: more than 1.25 times what that town's maker makes a second (`importShort`, 2026.11.22-8). The quarter of slack lets the workshop run a little short, which shows the player to raise the maker. Before this, the Falls' Telegraph Office ran short of wire during a wire boom and the Wire Mill's foreman bought 15 levels in three minutes on Gulch copper that fed about a third of them.
- Foremen keep working while you are away. The Automation panel (M4) grows Auto-build out of them.

### Charters and deeds
- A town's charter resets that town (buildings, towers, waves, research, foremen, and what it earned toward deeds) and pays deeds. The shared purse is kept (since save v12; before, the town's coins went back to its start coins). It keeps the explainers seen, the badges and the stats.
- Deeds paid (set with the balance bot; last changed 2026-11-17 when the game began in Dry Creek): 1 at best wave 33 and 1 more for every 5 waves past it, plus 1 at 85K coins the town earned itself in this charter and 1 more each time that grows tenfold. Coins an outpost earns count as the outpost's, never toward a town's deeds (before the purse they were paid into the town and filed under `town.fromOutposts`, which deeds still leave out), so a deed measures the town's own work. Earlier marks: wave 30 with 60K, then 52K, then 60K, all before the outpost fed the Gulch; with Dry Creek paying in and drawn on, the Gulch reached those 3-8 minutes sooner. The charter is offered once it would pay at least 1 deed (the active bot: at 37-42 minutes after the Gulch opens, from the coins).
- Deeds are kept for good. Each one adds 25% to the coins earned in every town (sales and bounties), as the named source "Deeds". (10% made a new charter barely faster than the old one.)
- **Your old towers.** A signed town also remembers which tower stood on which pad (`town.plan`, save v29). The goal strip asks for each one back on its old pad, highest level first: the building that makes a good it lacks, then the tower, and once the old kind can be paid, a low tower of another kind (under level 5, before its path) is swapped back with Sell. Without it the newcomer filled every Ridge pad with Crossbows right after the charter (the only tower its stone could pay for) and stalled at waves 44-47 for hours.
- **A goal behind a locked lot** asks for the level that opens it ("Tap the Sand Pit and upgrade it to level 6 to open the Glassworks"), even when its own check waits for the lot, once nothing else is on the strip; and a town whose goals wait that way is a place the strip sends the player to. Before it, every goal left in Silver Falls waited on a locked lot, no other place had one, and the strip was blank in every town for 6-9 minutes at a time (2026-11-25).
- **Saving for the next town.** Once the Capital stands, the save for the next town works like the Capital's: the strip asks for Hold waves first, and while only coins are missing it never sends the player to resume a town that lost while away.
- **Old ground.** A signed town remembers its best wave from before (`town.record`). Until it passes that wave again, its towers hit 3 times harder (the named source "Waves you beat before") and Call now works right away, so it races back. The bots showed why: even double income or kept research left the climb back at 45-75% of the first, because the wall near wave 25-30 is a damage wall and the waves come on a clock. Target: back to the old best wave in half the first climb or less (median over the seeds, no seed over two thirds).
- Deed perks come in pairs, and you pick one side of each pair once you hold enough deeds. A pick stays until the next charter, which clears every pick so you choose again (src/data/perks.js):

| Deeds | One side | The other side |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Boomtown: every building makes 20% more goods | Fort Town: every tower hits 20% harder |
| 2 | Big Guns: tower upgrades cost 30% fewer coins (few strong towers) | Many Hands: new towers cost 40% fewer coins (many cheap towers) |
| 3 | Quick Study: research takes half the time | Hard Bargain: goods sell for 15% more |
| 5 | Bounty Hunters: raiders drop 50% more coins | Stone Walls: the vault takes 50% more hits |
- The goal strip asks for a charter (clarity fix 9, 2026-11-24): once the charter is out, when the player would otherwise only wait for a climb, it asks for a town's first charter if that town is not the one the next place needs to climb (the Gulch while the Ridge climbs for the Capital), or for a charter whose deeds make up all the deeds the next place still needs (the Ridge for Silver Falls). In the town it says "Tap Perks, then tap Sign the charter to get 1 deed" (or "... for deeds to open Silver Falls" beside "1 / 5 deeds"), elsewhere "Tap Map, Copper Gulch, then sign its charter in Perks". A town that signed once is not asked again for one more deed, so no town is reset in a loop. The newcomer bot signs when it asks (the Gulch at 78-84 minutes on every seed). The Perks sheet starts with two plain lines: what starts over and what you keep, then what a deed does.
- The Perks button appears at the first charter offer. Its sheet shows the deeds the charter would pay now, the best wave and coins earned with the next deed's mark for each, what signing takes away, the deeds held and the pairs. Sign the charter and Pick each need a second tap.

- **Institutions**: Market Board, Rail Office, Bank, University, War Office (see Institutions and the Empire tree), and the Stock Exchange in M6. Each has its own branch in the Empire tree.
- **Names** are never reused across institutions, tabs, branches, laws or sliders. `tools/audit.js` fails two goods, buildings, towns, tower kinds, paths, raiders, research items or perks with one name.
- **Laws** come in three pairs:
  - Free Market vs Planned Economy;
  - Militia vs Standing Army;
  - Open Borders vs Protectionism.
- **Multipliers.** Every one is a named source in `src/core/mods.js`, with a published cap per stat. The "explain" view lists each part. `tools/audit.js` fails a perk, research item or core rule that puts a bonus on a stat with no cap.
- **The Convention** resets every town, the Capital, Influence, the tree, laws and shares. It keeps perks, badges and stats, and pays Legacy (×1.25 each).
  - Legacy perks open towns early, found the Capital pre-built, and give a starting Influence pot.
  - A second life must reach the first life's peak in under 30% of the time.

## Late-game depth (owner ideas, 2026-11-20; PLAN.md M6)
The owner: "This will really stretch out the playability of the game." Built in small steps, one or two towers at a time.
- **Second fork at level 20.** On top of its level-5 path, a tower picks one of two more upgrades at level 20, so each tower has 4 ways to finish. The Snake Oil Still comes first (shipped 2026.11.21-7): **Slow Rot** trades its normal poison and 30% of its hit for a poison worth 7% of its damage a second that never wears off until the raider falls, so raiders rot the whole road after it; **Last Call** keeps its poison and hits a hurt raider harder, from its normal hit on a fresh raider up to 3 times on one nearly down (until 2026.11.22-6 it hit harder by its place on the road, 0.4 at the entrance up to 3 at the vault; the owner found a bonus for a place too direct: "The strategy should be implied, not direct"). Raiders are hurt late on the road, so each side suits a part of it without the card saying so. Auto-towers never picks a fork: a tower at level 19 waits for the player, as one at level 4 does. A tower already at 20 or past it with no fork (raised before its kind had one) picks with its next level and keeps its levels (owner, 2026-11-22), and every later fork works the same way. The Cannon comes second (shipped 2026.11.21-9; the bots raise one Cannon to level 20 in Iron Ridge on every seed, while their Mortars stop at 15-17, so the Mortar waits for a later box): **Rocket Battery** fires a volley of 4 rockets, each at its own raider (the ones nearest the vault first), each with a small blast, at 0.65 of the hit, 1.6 times the reach and 0.55 times the shots; a rocket follows its raider until it lands. It wants raiders riding apart and long stretches of road in reach. **Big Shell** fires one huge shot at 2.2 times the hit, half the shots and a quarter wider blast, and hits any boss twice as hard, so it wants packed raiders and the boss waves. Runs of the waves on the bots' late Ridge and Falls split the pads about evenly between them (`tools/fork-runs.js`). The Crossbow comes third (owner, 2026-11-22: "We need level 20 options for crossbow"), on top of Repeater or Longbow: **Piercing Bolts** hits 0.7 as hard, but each bolt goes on through up to 3 raiders in a line behind the first (within half a unit of the line and 2.4 past the first), so it wants raiders riding in a row toward it, such as a rush of horsemen; **Sharpshooter** hits 3 times as hard at 0.45 times the shots and always aims at the raider in reach with the most health left, so its hits land on brutes and the boss and lose the Crossbow's bonus on horsemen. The runs split the pads 125 to 97, each of the 4 finishes winning some, and the side that wins a pad follows the road there: where more of the road runs straight at the pad, Piercing Bolts; where more road is in reach, Sharpshooter. The Frost Lens comes fourth (shipped 2026.11.24-2), on top of Deep Freeze or Brittle Ice. The lens led the late damage (30-37% against a 36% limit), so both sides trade its own hits for help to the towers round it: **Blizzard** reaches 1.5 times as far at 0.55 of the hit, so it chills a long stretch of road and spreads its path's freeze or brittle ice over it; **Cold Snap** slows 1.4 times as much (it takes 63% off a raider's speed, against 45%, and no slow takes more than 80%), the boss too, at 0.8 of the reach and half the hit, so raiders spend far longer in reach of every tower close by. Its pulse ring is drawn as wide as it reaches. Runs of the waves on the bots' late Ridge and Falls split the pads on every seed (Ridge 6 to 30, 26 to 10, 26 to 10; Falls 17 to 21, 22 to 16, 20 to 18, Blizzard first), judged by the raiders that got through and how far they got, not the lens's own damage, which neither side raises. The trade-off is always what it does against which raiders, never just a bigger number, and a fork's card says only what it does, in one short line, never where or when to use it.
- **Call to arms** (shipped 2026.11.21-10; `src/core/arms.js`, `src/data/arms.js`). The War Office's third upgrade, the Armory, opens it in every town (by the bots' pace about 6 hours in, between the Capital and Silver Falls). A To arms button joins the side buttons. Its sheet says which buildings send workers, what the town loses (the goods its buildings would make in the time, at today's prices, less what its own workshops would use up), how long they fight (40 sec) and when it can be called again (5 min after the call). A call stops every building and sends two workers from each built building to a post by the road next to it. Each post shoots once a second at a boss in reach, or else the raider nearest the vault, for a share of a plain raider's health on that wave that grows with the building's level ((level / 10) to the power 0.4, at most 3 times the level-10 shot). The button shows the fight's time, then the wait, in a fixed-width clock. Trade-off: the lost goods for one strong push, so it is for a boss gate or a wave about to break through, never every wave. A town can never hold with it alone: on its own it stops at most a third to half of a wave (`test/arms.test.js`), and at a boss gate it beats a war wagon up to about 1.4 times tougher than the towers alone can. A wave played away is never helped by it (the background battle plays without it). Auto has its own switch, Off by default: on, it calls the workers out when the boss is on the road or the vault is under half. The active bot calls it whenever a boss is on the road; `tools/balance.js` (armsCheck) holds the militia under 12% of a town's late damage.
- **The airfield (Thunder Mesa).** One per empire. Its planes patrol and join fights in any town, and fly urgent goods between towns; upgrades add planes, range and payload. The trade-off is where the planes go: a patrol over one town is not freight for another.

## Automation (first part shipped 2026-11-18)
One sheet per town, opened with the Auto button on the play screen (`src/core/automation.js`, `src/data/automation.js`, `autoSheet` in `src/ui/sheets.js`). The button comes out once the War Office or the University stands at the Capital, with a dot until the sheet is first opened and an explainer card the first time. Every switch starts Off, and each one only takes over a choice the player has made by hand.
- **Spending** (decision, replacing the first sketch's per-town spend share): like a foreman, each one buys only when the price is at most half the coins on hand ("Each one buys only when you have twice what it costs"), so with one purse half of it always stays, whoever spends. The idle target (`tools/idle.js`) checks that spending never passes that cap.
- **Foremen** (Auto-build): hired per building on its card (see Foremen). The sheet switches every hired foreman of the town on or off at once and says how many work.
- **Auto-towers**: opens with the War Office. One tower level per town every 3 minutes (each War Office upgrade makes the wait 0.85 times as long), always the cheapest raise of a tower that stands. It never builds a tower, and a tower at level 4 waits until the player picks its path. It pays coins and goods like the player.
- **Keep trying the boss** (owner, 2026-11-17; shipped 2026-11-18; on screen "Try the war wagon again", the town's own boss, since 2026.11.20-11): opens with Auto-towers, Off by default. A town that loses a boss wave (its boss got through, or its vault fell on that wave) holds its waves as always and pays the loss once. With the switch on, it starts its waves again by itself as soon as its towers are stronger than when it lost: any tower a level higher, or a new tower (`towerPower` in `src/core/automation.js`). It then plays back up the 2 waves and meets the boss again. Decision: it waits for a stronger tower instead of trying again at once, so every try costs one coin loss and meets better towers, never the same loss in a loop. Since 2026.11.22-2 it waits by playing the wave before the lost one again and again, for its bounty, instead of on hold (the idle Gulch wall: from about hour 3 of the 12-hour idle run the Gulch sat on hold 45-60 minutes an hour and earned nothing, so the idle growth gate sat within 5% for several releases; the owner asked for "the town drops back and keeps playing"). Playing again a wave it beat costs nothing (its towers only got stronger since), and if that wave falls too, the try moves down to it. It takes up every loss, in play too (an in-play vault never held the waves, so the town in play fell again and again at its wall). Auto-towers is what usually makes the raise; if it cannot (it is off, or every tower waits for its path), the town keeps playing the wave again and its map card asks the player to raise one. The map badge says Retry soon while it waits and Retrying while it climbs, with no red dot unless it is stuck. Since 2026.11.19-1 it also takes up a vault that fell on a plain wave while the player was away (`lostTry`): in the 24-hour idle run every seed's Gulch lost its vault on wave 26-33 and sat on hold for the whole day while Auto-towers kept raising its towers. Its switch keeps the owner's name; its line on the Auto sheet says "After a boss gets through or the vault falls".
- **Auto-research**: opens with the University. When the lab is idle it starts the cheapest next level of an item the player has researched at least once.
- The switches belong to the empire (`game.shared.auto`, save v16): a charter keeps them. They keep working out of play and while time away is caught up.
- **Before the Capital**, research is always a manual choice, which teaches it.
- **What an idle empire grows** (decision 2026-11-18, `tools/idle.js`): by the Capital every good sells far past its market's depth, so sales sit near the market's ceiling (the sum of price times depth over every good, with the price bonuses): 85-87% of it on every seed, the rest being goods the workshops use up (bricks) or do not make yet (wagons). A player who adds no new goods cannot raise that much; growing past it is what the next town is for. What Automation grows while the player is away is the waves (Auto-towers, and bounties with them) and research. So the idle target is: total income rises from each 3-hour block to the next over 12 hours, sales and contracts hold (every hour at least 95% of the first), no purchase takes more than half the coins on hand, and the Capital opens at 2.5-4 h. A careful player also tends the towns it is not playing once the Capital stands (`tendTowns` in `tools/balance.js`): one level a minute in each that pays back within 30 minutes, and a foreman along the chain, highest building first.

- Each town has badges for income, wave, vault and alerts, which pulse when it needs you.

A train has an engine at each end, so it never turns: it crosses to the other track just before the platform, waits and runs back. Goods both ways ride the same vehicle: a train's open cars carry crates in the colors of the goods the place sends on the way in, are emptied and loaded at the station, and carry the goods it gets back on the way out (empty if it gets none). The goods change vehicles at the Capital, so the road round the plaza stays a plain road. Every vehicle on a spoke runs one cycle offset by a headway that keeps it clear of the one ahead at the platform, a slot of the timetable starts or stops only while its vehicle is in the shed, and a vehicle shows only once wholly out of the shed, so nothing appears, vanishes or jumps in the open. When the Rail Office lays rails, the wagons out on the road finish their run first and then the trains start. The wagon road is lighter than a raider road, with two wheel ruts in each lane. Stations stand at least half a radian apart round the plaza (Silver Falls' and Sandy Bend's met it 0.9 apart, and their wagons turning there overlapped). `test/trains.test.js` steps every spoke for 4 minutes while the wanted count goes up and down and fails any vehicle off its lane, any that shows or goes outside its shed's roof, any jump, and any two of different trains that overlap. These dots used to sit on the town buttons at the top. Surveyed land shows its next rule ("Wave 20 in Iron Ridge", "15K coins", "Opens with Iron Ridge") or Ready to start with a dot.

## Onboarding
- **Tutorial**: spotlight steps through the first 30 minutes. It starts in Dry Creek Outpost, the smallest map (one camp, one tower, one loop), and carries on into Copper Gulch.
  - **Waves on hold** (Evaluation 2, 2026-11-19): waves never stay on hold with nothing on screen to start them again. Founding a town starts the waves of the town that paid for it (it held them to save the price, as the strip asked), with "The waves in Copper Gulch start again." on the toast; a hold from a loss stays. Before any other goal the strip says "Tap Resume waves" while the town in play holds (unless it saves for its next town), then "Go to Iron Ridge and tap Resume waves" for a town that lost while away and has Keep trying the boss off. `tools/firstplay.js` fails a town on hold over 10 minutes at a stretch while not saving.
  - **The goal strip** (`src/data/goals.js`): one short order at a time under Pause and Hold waves, with a pulsing gold frame on the pad, building tag or button it means. Dry Creek: build a Crossbow, Call now to bring wave 1 early (it costs nothing, comes at its normal size and pays bonus coins at once, since nothing else pays before wave 1, clarity fix 4), the Stone Camp to 2, the Copper Camp, 3 Crossbows, an upgrade, Call now (the next wave early), the Copper Camp to 5, a path, beat wave 10, go to Copper Gulch. The strip is never blank. It never asks for coins the purse lacks for more than 30 seconds while nothing comes in (clarity fix 4, `brokeWatch` in `src/core/goals.js`, `test/broke.test.js`), and the Research goal counts the coins its cheapest item takes. In a boss fight with the vault at 40% or less the strip stays on the fight: "Stop the war wagon. Skill pays. Waves come on a clock, and Call now lets a strong defense beat it. The active bot (and every strategy bot) calls the next wave once the last one's time, scaled up by how much bigger the called wave would be (1 + 2 x rush in the Gulch), is within 45% of the wave pace, nothing got through in the last two waves and the vault is above 60%, and never calls a boss wave early. The newcomer bot never calls on its own, only once when the goal strip asks. `tools/firstplay.js` (slow taps, 30% wrong buys, only the buttons that are out) must win Dry Creek and reach the Gulch's wave 10 within the newcomer limits, and its median times must be at least 20% slower than the active bot's on both.

**Saving for a town.** Founding a town is the one big purchase the player saves toward. Once the Gulch has beaten wave 20, the goal strip says "Tap Hold waves and save 15K coins for Iron Ridge", then "Tap Map, Iron Ridge, then Start Iron Ridge". The careful bots (`tools/balance.js`, `empire`) sign the Gulch's first charter when it pays a deed, climb back, and once the price is no more than 6 minutes of the Gulch's income they hold the waves, switch the foremen off and buy only buildings that pay back within 2 minutes until they can found it (about 6-10 minutes held). Then they win Pine Hollow and play the Ridge for an hour, raising the Gulch's Kiln (or what feeds it) when the Ridge runs short of bricks. The newcomer bot (`tools/firstplay.js`) follows the strip: it holds the waves when the strip says so and taps Start; it never signs a charter, so it gets there sooner (about 49-58 min). Decision: a player who skips the charter reaches the Ridge first, and one who signs it gets the deeds; both are fine.

**Pine Hollow on the strip** (2026-11-19). Once the Ridge has its Blast Furnace, its strip says "Tap Map, Pine Hollow, then Play Pine Hollow" until Pine Hollow is won. Pine Hollow's own strip asks for a Crossbow, the Clay Bank and 3 Crossbows, then "Beat wave 15 to win Pine Hollow", then "Tap Map, Iron Ridge, then Play Iron Ridge". ## Look and sound
- **The look pass (owner request, 2026-11-20; PLAN.md M5b, after the Silver Falls target).** Towns: varied ground with sand ripples, packed dirt round buildings, scrub, ruts and edge stones on the road; low hills or cliffs round each board; a little pooled life (townsfolk, a wagon, tumbleweeds, birds); buildings that grow at a few level marks; wooden or stone pad footings and soft contact shadows. The empire map: no beige tiles under towns, land that fits each town, routes as real roads and then rails with traffic, varied mesas, drifting cloud shadows, and locked sites as surveyed land with stakes and a tent. Costly parts are high tier only, and the phone frame stays in budget. Shipped so far (2026.11.21-2): ripples and grains in the ground's shader, packed dirt round the yards, grass tufts and edge stones, a rim of rock ledges round each board that sinks on the side the camera looks from, stone footings on the pads, and soft contact shadows under the pads, the vault and the buildings. Then (2026.11.21-3) the life: townsfolk walk between the built buildings and the vault, using the road as a street and going in and out at a door; a freight wagon (blue bed, cream cover, two horses, unlike the bandits' patched brown one) rides the road to a stop before the vault and back; tumbleweeds roll east in gusts; birds circle high. During a wave the townsfolk hurry indoors and the wagon leaves the road, so nothing on the road is mistaken for a raider. None of it can be tapped, and none of it stands on a pad, a building, the river or an entrance (`src/render/life-plan.js`, `test/life.test.js`). Graphics Low shows up to 3 people, the wagon and one tumbleweed; Medium up to 8 people, 2 tumbleweeds and 4 birds; High 12, 3 and 6. A small town has at most one more walker than it has doors. Then (2026.11.21-4) the buildings grow in steps, the same on every building: at level 10 crates and a barrel; at 25 two lanterns, and the windows light up (dark glass before); at 50 the main part of the building stands on two courses of laid stone (the top one set back, so they read apart) and grows a course taller, with a flag on the foundation's front corner, while yard props stay on the ground; at 100 a wide gold band over the line between the courses and a gold flag. The lanterns stand at the plot's front corners, clear of the barrel and the crate. A shop sign on its own post was tried and dropped (it read as a gallows or a road barrier) (`GROW` and `milestones` in `src/assets/models/gulch.js`). **Shadows** (2026.11.21-4): raiders have a soft round shadow under them on every tier, and they and the buildings' moving parts (wheels, hammers, carts, flags) cast no sun shadow, so the sun's shadow map is drawn only when a building or tower changes, a tower head turns, or an entrance sinks as the camera turns (`src/render/town-view.js`).
- **Models.** Low-poly 3D built in code, on vendored three.js.
  - One model file per town.
  - Each builder returns `{obj, movers, smoke, lights}` per tier, merged into one vertex-colored mesh per model, so several dioramas fit a phone's budget.
  - Every building, tower kind and raider has its own model; `tools/audit.js` fails one that would fall back to a stand-in.
- **Lighting.** Warm desert light, soft shadows, a day and night cycle, bloom on lamps and fires, and color grading.
- **Camera framing.** The home view fits the town's frame, grown to take in every pad, into the part of the screen the top bar and the goods strip leave free, close enough that every stone pad is at least 31 px across. On a short landscape or a narrow phone the ends of the town are a pan away, and the view slides sideways (at most 1.5 units) so no pad is cut by the screen's edge: each shows whole or sits off screen. A wide screen looks along the town's length from a lower angle. An entrance's walls sink to a third of their height when the camera looks across or into them, so they never hide the road or a pad. While a card covers the selected thing, the camera slides it into the free part, and slides back when the card closes unless the player moved the map. A short landscape screen puts the town name, the wave and Pause on one row with the goal under them. `tools/smoke.js` checks the pad size and the selection at every size.
- **Interface.** Crisp, flat pixel UI with hard 2px borders and pixel bevels. No glossy gradients or glass. Menu icons are rendered from the 3D models.
- **Sound.** Procedural Western music (WebAudio, no files) and synthesized sound effects (`src/audio/`).
  - Four tunes, written bar by bar from a seed so they never loop the same way: a lonesome whistle on the title; a calm build theme (boom-chick guitar, hooves on a wood block) between waves and on the empire map; a faster galloping tune while raiders are on the road; and a driving fiddle tune for a whole boss wave. A new tune starts on the next bar.
  - Effects: a hammer knock for every building, tower and upgrade; a shot sound per tower kind (crossbow twang, cannon boom, frost ping, still bubble, mortar thump and its shell landing); a coin blip for each bounty; a low horn when a boss wave starts; a thud when a raider gets through. Only the town in play makes sound, never catch-up, and a burst is thinned to at most 14 effects a second.
  - Music and Sound sliders in the Menu, kept under `cg-audio` (defaults 50% and 60%; 0 is Off). Sound starts on the first tap or key (browser rules) and the app in the background suspends it.
